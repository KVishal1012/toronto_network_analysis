# ASTER anonymous comprehension study

Recruit five distinct adults who have not helped build ASTER. Do not collect names, contact details or precise home addresses. Each person tests the same build listed in `session-template.json`. Keep actual responses under ignored `data/twin-qualification/<build_id>/`; never publish them.

Tell participants: “We are testing whether the interface communicates its evidence clearly. Please explain what you think it means; uncertainty is a valid answer.” Do not teach them the expected interpretations before recording their answers.

1. Open the local explorer, choose a region, search for a site and explicitly select it. Identify a planning record's source and retrieval date. Explain how certain its connection to this parcel is.
2. Read fixture A. What changed between the two observations? What can you conclude about physical work at the site?
3. Read fixture B. What can you conclude from the record being absent in the later export?
4. Compare fixture C with the candidate label in the interface. Explain the difference and what additional evidence you would want.
5. Find the baseline-only message. Explain why comparison is unavailable and whether the product establishes building energy performance.

Fixtures are invented test scenarios, not Toronto observations. They must never enter the registry, map or public package. Record the participant's own response verbatim, observed task problems and assessor decision for each task. Do not fill answers on their behalf.

The user recruits and conducts sessions. Codex can evaluate supplied responses but cannot act as a participant. Keep failed sessions. After design changes, conduct a fresh five-person evaluation of the new build; retain the previous build's results.
